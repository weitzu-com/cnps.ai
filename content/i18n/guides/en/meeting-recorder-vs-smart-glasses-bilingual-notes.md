# Meeting recorder vs smart glasses for bilingual meeting notes

Choose by form and consent optics first: a visible table or open-ear recorder fits rooms that need disclosed capture and a shared note workflow; smart glasses fit hands-free capture when policy and guests allow a wearable. Bilingual quality is proven on a scorecard, not by form factor. Run the Gulf meeting-notes pilot before bulk buy.

This page compares capture form — table or open-ear meeting recorder versus smart glasses — for Arabic–English meeting notes. It is not a catalog, not a brand league table, and not checkout. It does not invent microphone or battery scores. TicNote is one recorder family; listed glasses are a wearable category. Hardware waits until consent, capture, note and actions pass on real bilingual meetings.

## What both must do (consent → capture → note → actions)

Form is a means. Both shapes must finish the same four jobs, in order.

**Consent.** Calendar-invite disclosure plus a spoken start-of-meeting script. Stop if anyone objects. Repeat the script for late joiners. External guests hear the same notice. This is an operating protocol, not legal counsel and not an opinion on PDPL. Freeze the pack in the [consent and privacy test](https://www.cnps.ai/en/guides/gulf-meeting-consent-and-privacy-test) before the first file exists.

**Capture.** Collect audio the reviewer can use. Capture must be disclosed. Covert recording is out of scope, as are HR, legal, privileged and confidential-beyond-minutes sessions.

**Note.** The useful unit is the finished, checked note. Score Arabic, English and mid-sentence switches on meaning-critical errors and minutes-to-correct — not on a claimed word-error rate.

**Actions.** A person turns the checked note into lines with an owner and a due date. Owners confirm. A fluent summary that nobody owns is a fail.

A visible table device does not excuse a missing script. Hands-free glasses do not excuse a note that drops an Arabic condition inside an English sentence. Write the success rule before you compare SKUs. The [Gulf Arabic–English meeting-notes pilot](https://www.cnps.ai/en/guides/gulf-arabic-english-meeting-notes-pilot) owns that two-week protocol.

## Decision matrix: visibility, hands-free, guests, lectures, on-the-go

Match the room’s consent optics and the work of the note, then test quality. Do not rank brands. Do not fill empty cells with invented pickup range or battery hours.

| Situation | Table or open-ear recorder | Smart glasses | Pilot first? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Internal stand-up; shared note | Table unit is easy to see; open-ear needs extra spoken notice | Wearable only if policy and every participant accept it | Recorder unless the job is hands-free |
| Customer or partner guests | Guests can see a table unit; invite and script must name them | Wearable is easier to miss; guests must hear why it is on | Recorder unless wearables with guests are allowed |
| Lecture or one main speaker | Table or open-ear can sit in view; still disclose | Hands-free may help a moving instructor; still disclose | Match the instructor’s hands and the notice rule |
| On-the-go between rooms | Open-ear fits walking capture; keep the same access list | Glasses fit a named field task if capture is permitted | After the notes chain already passes in a room |
| Hands-free at a board or site | Open-ear keeps hands free with less “camera on a face” optics | Glasses when the wearer must see and capture without a table | Glasses only if policy, guests and the task require it |
| Mixed Gulf Arabic–English, including switches | Form does not decide quality — run the scorecard | Same scorecard, same ship rule | Neither form “wins” Arabic; the scorecard does |

A table device is a signal the room can point to. Open-ear and glasses are easier to miss, so the invite and spoken start must do more work. Hands-free is workflow fit, not a quality claim. If a guest objects, stop. A glasses trial that is really field instruction belongs on [one field task](/blogs/smart-glasses-one-field-task). This page stays on bilingual meeting notes.

## Bilingual and code-switch: form is not quality

A table microphone does not make Khaleeji-to-English switches correct. Glasses do not either. Do not buy on a brochure language toggle or a single percentage. Score meaning-critical errors in Arabic, English and at switch points, plus time-to-correct, on the same card for every form you trial.

Run the [Gulf Arabic–English meeting-notes pilot](https://www.cnps.ai/en/guides/gulf-arabic-english-meeting-notes-pilot). That page owns the two-week pack and the ship rule: consent and access complete, errors counted by language and by switch, correction inside the Week 0 budget, and at least one action with a named owner and a confirmed due date. Zero errors still fail if nobody can say who will do what by when.

Use the same samples if you trial both forms: an internal weekly, a guest session, a working session with mid-sentence switches, and an English-led update with Arabic asides. Keep files inside the access list. File any vendor language claim beside the scorecard. Leave the word-error box empty.

## Consent optics: table device versus wearable

Optics are how the room understands that capture is on. They are not a substitute for the written pack. A device on the table is not consent.

Open-ear capture is a special case in the [consent and privacy test](https://www.cnps.ai/en/guides/gulf-meeting-consent-and-privacy-test): if the room cannot see the device, speech has to carry the notice. Repeat the script when someone joins.

Smart glasses add a wearable on a face, sometimes with a camera or a display depending on the exact listing. Do not assume every pair records, displays or translates. Confirm the configuration you will trial. Nearby people must understand that capture is happening. If policy forbids a camera in the meeting room, a glasses SKU that includes a camera is the wrong form for that room — a policy gate, not a product ranking.

Stop-on-objection, late-join repeats, named access, retention and deletion proof apply to both forms. Vendor questions on residency, subprocessors, training use, export and deletion stay in writing. Escalate answers to counsel. This page does not interpret a statute.

## When to pilot which form first

Pilot the form that matches the rooms you will run in the next two weeks. Do not pilot both at once unless a reviewer can score two capture paths.

**Start with a table or open-ear recorder** when most sessions are disclosed team meetings, guests need a visible signal, or the note is a shared artifact. Stay device-agnostic at first if you already capture with consent. Add a dedicated recorder only when capture — not correction — is the bottleneck. After the workflow passes, open the [TicNote collection](https://shop.cnps.ai/collections/all-ticnote-products) and confirm the listing. This site does not rank Lite against Pods and does not take payment.

**Start with smart glasses** when the job is hands-free, policy and guests already allow a wearable, and you can name the meeting types. Keep the same consent and scorecard gates. Do not skip Week 0 because the device is worn. Listed glasses live on [Equip my team](/products) at category level; this method will not rank SKUs or invent a mic or battery score.

**Do not start with a bulk mix.** Pass one form on the bilingual scorecard, then decide whether a second form is a different job. Write a one-page decision: proceed, narrow the meeting types, change the capture path, or stop.
