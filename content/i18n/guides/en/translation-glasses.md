## What translating glasses actually do

All four products in this guide work the same way. Microphones in the frame pick up speech. A companion app on your phone, and for most languages a cloud service, turns it into text in another language. The difference is where that translation ends up:

- **In-lens captions.** A small monochrome display in the lenses shows the translated text in your line of sight, so you can keep looking at the speaker. INMO GO3 and Even G1 work this way.
- **Audio plus phone text.** The translation plays through open-ear speakers in the frame, and a transcript appears in the phone app. Ray-Ban Meta (Gen 3) and solos AirGo V2 work this way.

Glasses that translate languages mostly help the wearer. To reply, you usually need a second channel. INMO says the translation of your own speech is [spoken through your phone speaker or the INMO Speaker accessory](https://www.inmoxr.com/products/inmo-go3). Meta says your translated speech [appears as text in the Meta AI app for the other person to read](https://www.meta.com/help/ai-glasses/955732293123641/). Plan for that hand-off before you rely on the glasses at a counter or on a stand.

## How to read the table

### Language count is not the same as your language pair

A headline number tells you how many languages a list contains. It does not tell you whether *your* pair works in *both* directions, whether a language can be shown on the lens, or which accents were tested. The Even G1 FAQ, for example, lists Arabic as a language whose translated text cannot be displayed. Before you buy, check the manufacturer's current list for each language you need, as speech input and as translated output.

### Offline versus online

Most AI translation glasses need a data connection because the translation runs in the cloud. Offline modes cover a shorter list. INMO lists 9 offline languages on Android and 13 on iOS. Meta lists 6 offline languages once you have downloaded the packs. Even Realities says G1 translation needs the internet. solos does not say on the pages we checked. If you will work in exhibition halls, basements, aircraft or roaming zones, test with mobile data switched off.

### Captions in the lens versus audio and phone text

In-lens captions let you read while you keep eye contact. That suits noisy rooms and people who prefer reading to listening. Audio-first glasses keep your view clear and also handle music, calls and the camera, but in a fast conversation you have to listen to the translation. If you are hard of hearing, or the room is loud, captions in view are usually the deciding factor.

### Weight, fit and prescriptions

The published weights for INMO GO3 and Ray-Ban Meta (Gen 3) are both in the 50–60 g range. Even Realities and solos do not publish a weight on the pages we checked. If you wear prescription lenses, confirm how each brand handles them before ordering. The process and cost are different for each.

## A one-week test before you commit

1. **Write down your language pairs and directions.** For example: Japanese speech → English text, and English speech → Japanese audio for the other person.
2. **Pick one real setting.** A trade-show stand, a front desk or a sales visit. Avoid demo videos.
3. **Test online and offline.** Run the same conversation on good Wi-Fi and with data switched off.
4. **Time a full shift.** Note when the battery runs out and how you recharge or swap it.
5. **Check the reply channel.** Can the other person read or hear your side without passing your phone around?
6. **Ask about privacy and the camera.** Some sites restrict camera glasses. Check where recordings and transcripts are stored.

Run the same test on every model you are considering. Smart glasses work best when you start with one task. See [Smart glasses: choose one field task first](/blogs/smart-glasses-one-field-task).

## Common questions

### Do translation glasses work without internet?

Some do, for a shorter list of languages. INMO lists offline translation for 9 languages on Android and 13 on iOS (inmoxr.com product page). Meta lists 6 offline languages for Ray-Ban Meta once you download the language packs (Meta Help Center). Even Realities says Even G1 translation needs an internet connection because it uses cloud services (Even Support Center). solos does not state an offline mode on the pages we checked on 19 October 2026.

### Where does the translation appear?

On INMO GO3 and Even G1 it appears as text on a green Micro LED display in the lenses (manufacturer pages). On Ray-Ban Meta (Gen 3) you hear it through open-ear speakers and see a transcript in the Meta AI app (Meta product page and Help Center). On solos AirGo V2 it appears as text in the solos AirGo app, and Instant Translate adds audio (solos FAQ).

### How many languages do AI translation glasses support?

It depends on the model and on whether you are online. On 19 October 2026 the manufacturers listed 98+ online languages for INMO GO3, 22 for Even G1, 20 online for Ray-Ban Meta, and over 28 for solos AirGo V2. Check that your exact pair is supported in both directions.

### How much do translation glasses cost?

On 19 October 2026 the manufacturers' US stores listed INMO GO3 at $699.00 regular and $509.00 on sale, Even G1 at $599 (no longer available), Ray-Ban Meta (Gen 3) Wayfarer at $449 and solos AirGo V2 Prime 2 at $299.00, all in USD.
